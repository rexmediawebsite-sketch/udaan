Still Frame

gave the world beauty born from the silence of empty studios.

Q&A

Welcome Maren. So how did Still Frame begin its journey?

Less than a year into launching the gallery, everything shut down. I had to close our doors, cancel every exhibit, and rethink it all. But I never stopped curating because I was so determined not to let the artists’ momentum die. We hit the ground running to build a digital space, and we’ve been evolving since.

How did you know where to begin?

I didn't wait until we had the perfect platform, I saw artists struggling, isolated, uninspired, overwhelmed, and set to the task of creating ways to share their work with the world as quickly as possible.

What was the initial reaction?

We had so many people writing and reaching out that the online exhibits and archived works saved them in isolation. The atmosphere was so intimate, and it was really powerful to have people connecting through art, even though we were all in our own rooms, in different cities.

"Art wasn't an escape from reality—it became our only truthful anchor to each other."

We were one of the first galleries to launch a virtual exhibition after the shutdown. I think that, they saw how hard we fought for them, they trusted us while we continued to refine the digital experience.

There’s a feeling of urgency like — this is our one life, our one chance, we don’t have time to be indifferent anymore. We’re gonna create for a better world, we’re gonna create to reclaim our voice in this life, and we’re gonna create because we deserve to feel beauty and wonder.
